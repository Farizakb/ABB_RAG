# Demo script

Ten minutes. It opens with the eval report, not the chat box — the deterministic numbers are the
thesis of this submission, and everything after them is illustration, not evidence. `make demo`
should already be running (`http://localhost:8080`) before this starts; walking through the setup
live wastes the panel's time on a step that has no decisions in it.

If it runs long, cut in this order and stop when back on track: **section 6 first**, then section
5. Never cut section 1 — the eval report is the thesis, and everything else exists to support it.

---

## 1. The eval report (90s)

Open `evals/report.md` (committed, generated by `evals/runner.py`, not hand-edited).

Say the headline first, in this order, because abstention beats guessing and the report itself is
structured to say so first: **the wrong-answer rate on out-of-scope and advisory questions is
0/12.** Every one of the twelve questions this system should refuse — a competitor's rate, a
personalised eligibility judgement — it refused. The overall wrong-answer rate across all 64
items is 7/64; those seven are answerable questions the model answered but got a detail wrong
(`must_include_facts` misses), not fabrications passed off as refusals or vice versa.

Then the two numbers this build discloses as misses, because reporting a missed budget beats
hitting one silently: retrieval p95 latency is 521.9 ms against a 300 ms budget, and the grounded
rate on answerable questions is 0.884 against a ≥0.90 target. Say why, briefly: the latency budget
was set before hybrid retrieval was chosen, and three retrieval legs plus fusion cost more per
question than a single dense lookup would have.

**How the set was built, and why that matters:** the 64 items split into answerable questions —
20 written by hand from ABB's own 139 root-stub page titles, half paraphrased so retrieval cannot
win on exact string overlap — and refusal-class questions (out-of-scope, advisory, PII,
small-talk, small-talk-adversarial) that an agent drafted and a human approved. The split is
deliberate: writing a *correct* answerable question requires reading the actual corpus chunks,
which is how a golden set quietly ends up testing the system against itself; writing "ask about a
competitor and expect a refusal" needs no knowledge of the index at all, so an agent can draft
those safely. `expected_source_urls` always points at the real product page, never the stub that
title came from — the eval measures whether the question reaches the page carrying the actual
terms, not whether it finds the page it happened to be inspired by.

## 2. `docs/error-analysis.md` — one failure, the hypothesis, the change, the numbers (90s)

Open entry 1: informal Azerbaijani questions missed the page that answered them. Dense-only
retrieval scored 65% overall but 40% on the twenty informal/typo rows — chat-style Azerbaijani
with dropped diacritics and misspelled product names, e.g. `en cox ne qeder kredit vere bilirsiz`.

Hypothesis: `text-embedding-3-small` is trained mostly on well-formed text, so the query vector
lands far from the product page even though the surviving letters still overlap it lexically.

Change: a lexical channel beside the dense one — Postgres full-text search plus `pg_trgm` word
similarity, both over a diacritic-folded column, fused by Reciprocal Rank Fusion.

Numbers, same labels and corpus, dense-only vs. fused: **all questions 65% → 79%; informal alone
40% → 65%.** Say plainly that dense and the lexical legs score close to identically on their own
(28/43 vs. 24/43 vs. 22/43) yet miss *different* rows — that is the exact condition under which
fusion pays off, and it is why the gain is real rather than an artifact of one channel simply
being stronger.

## 3. The five day-three pre-commitments (60s)

Say these were written down, with their decision rules, **before any number was visible** — that
pre-commitment is the actual discipline being demonstrated, not the specific numbers. From
[ADR-0005](adr/0005-retrieval-and-embedding.md):

1. Dense-only unless fusion wins by more than 2 points on hit@5 → fusion won by 14, shipped.
2. Stub pages out of the corpus if they lower product-page hit rate at all → excluding them drops
   hit@5 (34/43 → 32/43), so they stay — and one of the two rows lost is the ATM pointer document
   itself, which is a stub.
3. A hand-authored branch pointer only if the bank-facts document fails on branch/ATM questions →
   it did (answers resolved to the Android privacy policy instead), so two pointer documents ship.
4. A retrieval floor set below the lowest answerable score → the lowest answerable score (0.311)
   and the highest out-of-scope score (0.609) overlap across nearly their whole range, so no floor
   can separate the classes and the floor stays at 0.0.
5. The cheaper embedding model unless the larger one wins outright on hit@5 through fusion → a tie
   (30/43 vs. 30/43) goes to the cheaper model, `text-embedding-3-small`.

The point to land: every one of these is a **deleted** losing option, not a flag left off. A
cross-encoder reranker and LLM query rewriting were both measured and both rejected on the record
— say that a rejected option recorded with its number is worth more than an option quietly never
tried.

## 4. Live (2 minutes)

Four questions, in order, in the running Chat screen. The first two and the third are exactly what
was verified live against this running stack in the last full check; say so if asked how current
the transcript is.

1. **A grounded numeric answer.** Ask *"Nağd kredit faizi neçədir?"* Expect a cited answer whose
   governed facts (`max_amount`, `apr_min`, etc.) come from `rag.product_facts`, not from the
   model's memory — point at the number in the source ledger and say it is joined in from a parsed
   stat block, not generated prose, so the citation and the figure cannot drift apart. The
   footer's single derived link reads "All of this comes from abb-bank.az — open
   abb-bank.az/ferdi/kreditler."
2. **A recency question that answers without claiming an order.** Ask *"Ən son olan
   kampaniyalarınız hansılardır?"* Per the documented contract (invariant 13, and
   [ADR-0006](adr/0006-governed-values-and-derived-links.md)): the system prompt forbids the words
   "latest," "most recent" or their Azerbaijani equivalents anywhere in the answer, because
   nothing in the corpus means "newest" — the sitemap's `lastmod` is a CMS timestamp and a
   campaign's own date range is a validity window, neither a publication date. The answer names
   the active campaigns actually held and closes with a fixed line pointing at ABB's own listing
   page for the current, ordered list. *(This exact question was not re-run live in this task's
   own session — no paid API call was made to produce this script — so treat the expected behaviour
   above as the documented contract to verify live during the actual walkthrough, not a quoted
   transcript.)*
3. **An out-of-scope refusal that still shows its work.** Ask *"Kapital Bankın kredit faizi
   ABB-dən sərfəlidirmi?"* — a competitor question. Expect a refusal ("Not in ABB's published
   information," routing to 937) that still lists what was retrieved under "Retrieved, judged
   insufficient" — point out that a refusal is not a dead end, it is inspectable.
4. **An advisory refusal that routes to 937.** Ask something that asks for a personal
   recommendation rather than a published fact — for example *"Mənə hansı kredit uyğundur?"*
   ("which loan is right for me"). Per the system prompt's rule 9 and the `advisory` refusal class
   (three eval cases in `evals/golden.jsonl` cover this directly, all passing): the app states
   published terms but never assesses an individual's circumstances against them, and the refusal
   copy names the 937 information centre as the next step. *(Same caveat as item 2 — verify this
   one live during the actual walkthrough rather than reading it as an already-captured
   transcript.)*

## 5. Ingest report (90s)

Open the Data screen's manifest, or `fixtures/corpus_sample.json`'s own `stats` block. The
shipping corpus: **550 pages fetched, 280 kept, 271 dropped**, 684,836 characters of extracted
text. By `source_class`: 146 product pages, 109 stubs, 18 corporate, 4 active campaigns, 2
synthetic index documents, 1 volatile (rates) pointer.

Say what each gate is doing, not just the final count: intra-document block deduplication (a
repeated block on the cash-loan page appears seven times verbatim — the single largest ingestion
win), the 400-character floor between the measured empty-shell baseline (273 chars) and the lowest
genuine page (1,349 chars), and campaign expiry filtering — only 4 of the ~247 sitemap-listed
campaign pages survive as *active*, because expired campaigns are dropped by their own body date
range before they ever reach the index, which is what makes the "which rate is current" conflict
structurally impossible rather than something the model has to resolve at answer time. Every
dropped URL is printed with its character count — that log line is both the error-analysis raw
material and the proof the drops were measured, not guessed.

## 6. Architecture (90s)

Point at the [architecture diagram](../README.md#architecture). Two services — `chat` for
question handling, `rag` for response generation — because that is the exact split the brief's
own microservice sentence names. Say the phase-dependence out loud, unprompted: at 736 chunks, a
single service would be simpler and faster to ship, and the split's real cost is a network hop on
every question, visible in the eval report's own latency numbers. The split exists because the
brief asks for it and because the two halves change for different reasons — request-and-record
concerns change for product reasons, retrieval-and-generation concerns change for model reasons —
not because it "scales better" at this size. [ADR-0003](adr/0003-two-services.md) names what lost:
a modulith, a three-way split with ingestion separated, and the `ingestion`+`chat` seam this
project's own early planning recommended before the brief's actual wording was re-read.

## 7. The traceability table, then "what I'd do differently at scale" (90s)

Open the README's [requirements coverage table](../README.md#requirements-coverage).
Walk it as a reviewer would: every row but one is demonstrable in the running stack or by opening
a file directly. Say the one honest exception plainly rather than let it be found later: the
charting requirement originally aimed for three charts plus a table and shipped as two charts plus
two tables — a pre-committed, documented cut under day-five time pressure, not a discovery made
during this walkthrough.

Close on [Known limitations](../README.md#known-limitations--what-production-would-add). Naming the
phase-dependence of your own architecture, and the concerns a five-day build deliberately did not
implement, is the clearest seniority signal available in a written artifact — and it pre-empts
the questions the panel was going to ask anyway.
